# Evidence, currentness and source roles

Resolve the exact person, opportunity, document and task before reusing material.
The same company, title or wording can refer to different applications or sources.
Keep relevant source links, message/document IDs and available exact revisions.

## Distinct evidence lanes

- Career facts: actual history supported by the user's statements or appropriate
  primary records. Preserve the actor, action, object, authority, period, scale,
  beneficiary, result, causality and certainty.
- Current intent: what the user now wants to pursue, emphasize or avoid. It is not
  a claim about past behavior and does not automatically belong in employer copy.
- Voice: genuinely authored writing, or examples the user selected with context.
  Selection is not pure authorship; pasted/generated text is not automatically a
  reliable personal-voice sample.
- Corrections: exact user instructions with their original task and surrounding
  text. Apply them at the appropriate level; keep scope and exceptions visible.
- Application state: submitted versus working artifacts, application status,
  interviews, deadlines and decisions. Drafting or discussing an action does not
  prove the action happened. Submission or a later interview does not establish
  writing quality, authorship or the user's endorsement of a passage.

For mixed documents, classify the relevant passage instead of assigning the whole
file one authority. A generated sample, interview answer, hypothesis or draft may
be useful writing but is not new career history. A newly mentioned factual detail
remains tentative until the user clearly confirms it as actual history in context.

## Current use is different from retained history

An old source or assessment can be retained accurately while no longer supporting
current action. Check changed source versions, corrections, intended purpose,
subject, application scope and effective dates before reuse. A later instruction
can supersede an inference without erasing the original conversation.

Where an available runtime supplies currentness/readiness checks, use the exact
source and target revisions and honor blocked or stale results. Without such a
runtime, perform the available source comparison and describe its real scope; do
not pretend that a technical gate ran. Changed evidence may require a new draft
or assessment, but do not silently rewrite historical judgments or regrade jobs.

Keep uncertainty proportionate. Absence from accessible evidence is not proof the
user lacks an experience. A plausible relationship between roles is still an
interpretation unless the sources establish it. Required unknowns need a targeted
question; optional contested detail can often be omitted.

Never weaken a qualification, denominator, period, attribution or uncertainty to
make a number fit. Prefer omitting an unnecessary detail to making it misleading.
An exact quotation validates the quoted words, not the interpretation or truth of
all claims attributed to them. Preserve context when it changes meaning.
