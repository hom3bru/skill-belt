# A measured professional case

Keep supported career scope, current intent and the role-specific proposition
separate. Establish what the candidate contributes before choosing which examples
best support that point. Do not select an example merely because it is vivid.

## Choose the right unit

Use sustained remit when describing a recurring responsibility; portfolio when the
relationship to a defined group is supported; a program when there was a structured
program; a specific event when the question or achievement needs that occurrence.
A career pattern needs support across independent roles or periods. Similar words
in copied resumes do not establish a recurring pattern.

Retain accurate support, coordination, advice, oversight or implementation roles.
Do not promote support into decision authority, claim sole ownership of joint work
or infer causality from a later organizational outcome. Equally, do not diminish
real scope merely to sound modest. Concrete activities, beneficiaries, constraints
and decisions can make a contribution clear without a statistic.

Ask: what supported work connects these experiences, what differs between them,
and why does this matter for the present role? Avoid inventing a seamless career
arc, reasons for a move or a longstanding personal mission.

## Select and arrange

State the intended reader conclusion in plain language. Assign each paragraph a
function and the source-linked support that earns its place. Select relevant role
criteria to support the case; do not cram every criterion into each paragraph or
answer. Explicitly required subparts still need answers. Useful relationships
include explanation, complement, contrast, qualification and progression. These
are relationships between ideas, not mandatory transition words.

Read adjacent paragraphs without their transition phrases. Does the relationship
still follow? If paragraphs can be moved anywhere, the draft may be an inventory.
If the proposition could fit any applicant, it may be generic. If an incident
occupies most of the letter, ask whether it displaces the stronger sustained remit.
Do not fix either problem by adding generic strategy or leadership adjectives.

When two genuinely different approaches are plausible, compare their support and
fit to the brief. Usually deliver one recommendation. An unchosen angle is not
permanently rejected; selection is contextual.

## Detail must earn its space

A verified statistic is eligible evidence, not an instruction to add it. Compare
the complete draft with and without an optional number or event. Retain it when it
substantially strengthens the requested case; omit it when its explanation crowds
out the argument. Keep every qualification needed for truth if it stays.

This is not an anti-anecdote or anti-metric rule. A behavioral question needs a
specific incident. A form requesting counts needs the supported count. An event
can be a strong resume achievement. Match the unit to the task rather than
applying a universal template or numerical word-share rule.

## Finish the actual argument

Check the opening and ending against current intent. Ensure the middle advances
the proposition with measured, supported assertions. Reconsider selection and
proportion before polishing sentences. Preserve strong paragraphs and distinctive
wording the user endorses; do not flatten personality merely to sound smoother.
When the middle is the weakness, repair its argument rather than reflexively
conventionalizing an effective opening or closing. A shorter or more embellished
version is not automatically better.
