# Revise at the cause

Read the requested passage in context. Identify the desired change and protected
facts, scope, text and layout. A one-word change can alter authority or causality.

For a bounded edit, preserve the surrounding text and comply with the stated
length/scope constraint. Compare the actual before and after, not a recollection.
If another consequential issue is outside scope, flag it rather than silently
rewriting the whole document. No change is a valid recommendation when the text
already does the requested job.

For structural rescue, revisit the professional proposition, evidence unit and
paragraph relationships. Shortening every role summary rarely fixes a resume-like
letter. Another detailed incident does not fix a request to represent sustained
remit. A wrong opening may require a different case, not a punchier sentence.

## Interpret feedback accurately

Distinguish corrections to facts, ownership, subject, intent, relevance, scale,
structure, stance, wording, length and mechanics. Apply the current instruction
immediately. Reopen dependent claims, selection and argument when the cause is
factual or structural; do not hide it under a general tone adjustment.

After correcting a fact, review the complete draft again. A newly usable detail
may still be unnecessary. A longer explanation can be accurate and still distort
the user's intended professional direction. Preserve sound surrounding text.

## Learn from writing without overgeneralizing

Compare the nearest relevant whole-writing examples by purpose, audience, period
and actual authorship or selection status. Extract a specific supported decision,
not a vague list of style adjectives. Retain the contextual evidence and check a
counterexample when available. A letter may lead with contribution while an
application form properly requires dates, titles and an evidence-heavy chronology.

With one example, treat transfer as provisional. Explicit current directions
outrank inferred preferences. Silence is not approval. A contextual choice is not
a permanent preference unless the user says so or sufficiently repeated authentic
contexts support it. Keep exceptions rather than forcing one universal template.

Test uptake in the next actual draft: did the proposition, selection, proportion
or paragraph relationship change where needed? Merely retrieving an old correction
is not proof it was applied. Do not ask for more biography as a substitute for
using a correction already supplied.
