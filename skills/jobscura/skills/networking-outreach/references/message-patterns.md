# Message patterns

These are structures, not scripts. Fill them only with verified facts, and keep the user's own
voice. Aim for under 120 words unless the channel or the relationship calls for more.

| Message | Structure | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Referral request (someone the user knows) | Reconnect line with a real shared context, then the role and its link, why the user fits (2-3 lines from evidence), the ask ("would you be comfortable referring me, or pointing me to the right person?") and an easy out | The referrer stakes some reputation, so include a short case they can forward unchanged |
| Informational ask (cold or weak tie) | Why this person (a specific, sourced reason), who the user is in one line, a small time-bound ask (15-20 minutes) and an easy out | Do not disguise a job request as curiosity |
| Recruiter introduction | Role link or target area, a 2-line fit summary from evidence, availability or constraints if relevant, and the next step | Match the recruiter's stated process |
| Application follow-up | Role and date applied, one new relevant point if there is one, a short interest reaffirmation and a polite question about the timeline | Send at most one, timed after the stated timeline; never imply a competing offer that does not exist |
| Post-interview thank-you | Thanks with a specific thing discussed, one line reinforcing fit or clarifying an answer, and continued interest | Only refer to what was actually discussed |
| Withdrawal | A short thank-you, a clear withdrawal and an optional door left open | No over-explaining |
| Reply to a rejection | Thanks, a request for brief feedback if wanted, and interest in future roles | Keep it gracious; no argument |
| Holding reply to an offer | Thanks, confirmation of the written terms, a request for the decision deadline and the time to review | Hand over to [offer-negotiation](../../offer-negotiation/SKILL.md) for the substance |

## Personalisation that is honest

- Good: "Your team's spring workshop series (announced 12 March) is close to the outreach work I've
  run for three years." It is specific, sourced, dated and relevant.
- Bad: "I've long admired your work." It is generic, unverifiable and often false.
- Do not mention personal details about the recipient found outside professional channels.

## Channel and timing

Use the channel the person or employer prefers. Work email or a professional network beats a
personal number. Note the recipient's time zone when suggesting a send time. Bulk or automated
sending is out of scope.
