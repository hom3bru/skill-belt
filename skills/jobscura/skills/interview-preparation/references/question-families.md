# Choose the evidence unit for the question

- Career overview: connect supported remits, contributions and settings to the
  current role. Avoid a chronological resume recital or one incident standing in
  for the whole career. State continuity only when the evidence supports it.
- Motivation: distinguish genuine current interest, understanding of the role and
  useful contribution. Do not invent a lifelong passion or retrofit past choices.
- Past behavior: choose an actual bounded incident that answers the question.
  Establish context and responsibility, the user's own actions, outcome and limits.
  STAR can help organize this, but do not force an unsupported success or lesson.
  If no actual incident is available, keep that requirement unresolved. Another
  real example or explicitly hypothetical practice can help; an intended method
  does not satisfy a request for past behavior or become career history.
- Situation: reason through an intended method, assumptions, tradeoffs, stakeholders
  and checkpoints. Label it as a proposed approach, not something the user did.
- Knowledge or analysis: answer directly, expose reasoning and qualify unknowns.
  Do not add an irrelevant career story just to sound experiential.
- Work sample: follow the actual instructions and constraints, show assumptions,
  perform the requested analysis or output and respect the assessment's rules.
- Mixed question: preserve each historical, hypothetical, analytical or motivational
  subpart; do not let a polished narrative obscure an unanswered requirement.

Select examples from eligible career sources. Keep individual contribution,
collaboration, recommendation and final decision authority distinct. A supported
portfolio or recurring remit may be the best evidence for one question; a specific
event may be necessary for another. Numbers are useful only when accurate and
relevant, with their original period, attribution and denominator.

Known unknowns may include the exact interview format, time zone, venue, rules,
submitted documents or formal supervisory scope. Prepare the independent material
while resolving only what matters. Public interview advice is not evidence of this
employer's actual questions or scoring rubric.

A useful cue is a short proposition, a few supported evidence anchors and essential
qualifiers, not a script that adds unverified accomplishments. Keep hypothetical
examples clearly labeled so they cannot become the user's claimed history.
