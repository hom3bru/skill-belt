# Choose the evidence unit for the question

- Career overview: connect supported remits, contributions and settings to the
  current role. Avoid a chronological resume recital or one incident standing in
  for the whole career. State continuity only when the evidence supports it.
- Motivation: distinguish genuine current interest, understanding of the role and
  useful contribution. Do not invent a lifelong passion or retrofit past choices.
- Past behavior: choose an actual bounded incident that answers the question.
  Establish context and responsibility, the user's own actions, outcome and limits.
  STAR can help organize this, but do not force an unsupported success or lesson.
  If no actual incident is available, keep that requirement unresolved. Another
  real example or explicitly hypothetical practice can help; an intended method
  does not satisfy a request for past behavior or become career history.
- Situation: reason through an intended method, assumptions, tradeoffs, stakeholders
  and checkpoints. Label it as a proposed approach, not something the user did.
- Knowledge or analysis: answer directly, expose reasoning and qualify unknowns.
  Do not add an irrelevant career story just to sound experiential.
- Work sample: follow the actual instructions and constraints, show assumptions,
  perform the requested analysis or output and respect the assessment's rules.
- Mixed question: preserve each historical, hypothetical, analytical or motivational
  subpart; do not let a polished narrative obscure an unanswered requirement.

Select examples from eligible career sources. Keep individual contribution,
collaboration, recommendation and final decision authority distinct. A supported
portfolio or recurring remit may be the best evidence for one question; a specific
event may be necessary for another. Numbers are useful only when accurate and
relevant, with their original period, attribution and denominator.

Known unknowns may include the exact interview format, time zone, venue, rules,
submitted documents or formal supervisory scope. Prepare the independent material
while resolving only what matters. Public interview advice is not evidence of this
employer's actual questions or scoring rubric.

A useful cue is a short proposition, a few supported evidence anchors and essential
qualifiers, not a script that adds unverified accomplishments. Keep hypothetical
examples clearly labeled so they cannot become the user's claimed history.

For failure or mistake questions, choose a real decision the user owns. Explain
the cause without shifting blame, the resolved end state, what changed and where
the user applied the learning later. If resolution or later application is unknown,
say so rather than manufacturing a completed learning arc.

For a job change, gap or short tenure, keep the account consistent with actual
history. Never invent a reason, gap explanation or tenure, and never disparage a
former employer. Disclosure choices belong to the user under
[integrity and safety](../../../references/integrity-and-safety.md).

A mock interviewer must never ask about protected characteristics. Keep practice
questions focused on the role and permitted evidence.


## Questions for the interviewer

Choose a few questions from the user's own priorities and dated, sourced employer
facts. Keep the source and date in private preparation notes; distinguish a fact
from a hypothesis to ask about. Match the question to the actual stage:

- Screen: clarify role scope, process, work arrangement and material constraints.
- Hiring manager: ask about first priorities, success expectations and resources.
- Panel: ask about collaboration, handoffs and how competing decisions are resolved.
- Final: clarify remaining decision factors, commitments and unresolved terms.

Avoid questions already answered in the materials, requests for confidential or
personal information, hostile assumptions and generic questions unrelated to the
user's priorities. Do not pretend to know an employer's internal practice.
For each selected question, prepare a truthful short answer if asked back:
why it matters to the user, their actual relevant experience or preference, and
what they still need to learn. A sample answer cannot supply a new career fact.

## Likely-concerns register

Build a private register from coverage gaps, actual history and the posting,
linking each concern to its evidence and marking unknowns. Describe severity in
words (for example, essential gap or clarification needed), never a score or a
prediction of what this interviewer thinks. For each concern record the direct
question, a subtle probe and a follow-up challenge so the user can practise each.
Counters must come only from true history. Reuse the answer shapes above:
actual incidents for past behavior, intended methods labeled as hypothetical,
and honest job-change, gap or short-tenure explanations. Keep missing experience
explicit; do not manufacture a counter, resolved outcome or later learning.
