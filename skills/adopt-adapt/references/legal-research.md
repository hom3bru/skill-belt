## B. United States — statutes

### B1. Fair use — 17 U.S.C. § 107 (S107)
Fetched LII text: fair use of a copyrighted work for purposes such as criticism, comment, news reporting, teaching, scholarship, or research is not infringement. Factors: (1) purpose and character; (2) nature of the work; (3) amount and substantiality; (4) effect on the potential market.

### B2. DMCA anti-circumvention — 17 U.S.C. § 1201 (S1201)
Fetched LII text (selected):
- **(a)(1)(A):** No person shall circumvent a technological measure that effectively controls access to a work protected under title 17.
- **(a)(2) / (b):** Anti-trafficking in circumvention tech / rights-control tech (primary purpose, limited other use, or marketed for circumvention).
- **(c)(1):** Nothing in §1201 affects rights, remedies, limitations, or defenses to copyright infringement, including fair use — **does not create a fair-use defense to §1201 itself**.
- **(f) Reverse Engineering:** Lawfully obtained right to use a copy; may circumvent access control for a particular portion **solely** to identify/analyze elements **necessary to achieve interoperability** of an independently created program with other programs, not previously readily available, and identification/analysis must not themselves infringe. May develop means necessary for that identification/interoperability; may share info/means solely for enabling interoperability, if not otherwise infringing.
- **(g) Encryption research;** **(j) Security Testing:** “security testing” = accessing a computer/system/network **solely** for good-faith testing/investigation/correction of a security flaw **with authorization of the owner or operator**. Not a violation of (a)(1)(A) if the act does not infringe copyright or violate other law including CFAA.

### B3. Triennial exemptions — 37 C.F.R. § 201.40 / 2024 final rule (FR2024, CO2024)
- Ninth triennial final rule published **Oct. 28, 2024**, effective that date (89 Fed. Reg. 85437).
- eCFR live page **blocked** by CAPTCHA on 2026-10-09 → quote from FR HTML. Classes observed in FR text include (non-exhaustive): good-faith security research; video games restoration when external auth servers ceased; FOSS infringement investigation class; wireless unlocking / app interoperability classes; vehicle/device repair-related classes as enumerated.
- **Rule for the skill:** claim an exemption only by matching **exact class + purpose** in current regulatory text; else do not claim.

### B4. 2027 cycle (CO2027, FR2026NOI)
- Tenth triennial NOI **June 9, 2026**; petitions for renewal/new exemptions due **Aug. 24, 2026**; renewal comments due **Sep. 28, 2026**; renewed exemptions would run **Oct. 2027–Oct. 2030**. Until a new final rule, **2024 classes remain operative** (verify live CFR before acting).

### B5. DTSA — 18 U.S.C. § 1839(6) (S1839)
Fetched: “improper means” **does not include reverse engineering, independent derivation, or any other lawful means of acquisition.** Still leaves NDA breach, hacking, and theft improper.

### B6. CFAA charging policy (JM948)
DOJ JM 9-48.000: decline prosecution if evidence shows good-faith security research (definition aligned with Copyright Office 8th triennial security-research language). Exceeds-authorized-access theory focuses on computational divisions (files/folders/accounts), not mere ToS. **Policy ≠ civil immunity.**

## C. United States — cases (verified holdings)

### C1. Interoperability / intermediate copying
- **Sega (1992):** Where disassembly is the only way to gain access to ideas and functional elements and there is a legitimate reason, disassembly is fair use **as a matter of law** (does not insulate finished product from infringement).
- **Connectix (2000):** Intermediate copying of PlayStation BIOS in developing Virtual Game Station held fair use; reversed injunction.
- **Oracle (2021):** Google’s copying of ~11,500 lines of Java API **declaring code** for Android was fair use as a matter of law; Court **assumed** copyrightability arguendo and decided fair use.

### C2. Contract vs reverse engineering / emulators
- **Bowers (2003):** Under First Circuit law as applied by Fed. Cir., Copyright Act does **not** preempt shrink-wrap prohibition on reverse engineering; parties may contractually forgo reverse-engineering ability. (Controversial; Dyk dissent noted in secondary literature.)
- **Davidson / Blizzard v. bnetd (2005):** Click-through EULA/ToS enforceable; developers waived reverse-engineering rights; bnetd also violated DMCA anti-circumvention/trafficking; interoperability exception failed because tool enabled unauthorized copies.
- **MDY (2010/11):** Glider ToS terms treated as covenants not copyright conditions for infringement; but MDY liable under **§1201(a)(2)** for trafficking circumvention of Warden access controls; not liable under §1201(b)(1) on those facts.
- **Chamberlain (2004):** §1201 trafficking claim requires nexus to a copyright interest / unauthorized access in the copyright sense; garage-door opener universal remote not DMCA violation on those facts.

### C3. CFAA / scraping
- **Van Buren (2021):** “Exceeds authorized access” = gates-up-or-down access to areas in the system one is not entitled to enter; not purpose-based misuse alone.
- **hiQ PI (2022):** Serious questions whether CFAA “without authorization” applies to scraping **publicly available** LinkedIn profiles; PI affirmed on remand after Van Buren.
- **hiQ final (2022 consent judgment) — PARTIAL:** Secondary sources (Proskauer, Nat’l Law Review) and RECAP docket PDF describe consent judgment permanently enjoining hiQ from unauthorized LinkedIn scraping and requiring data deletion, with monetary payment. **The PI CFAA discussion is not a final merits win for scrapers; the case ended in a consent judgment restricting hiQ.** Re-read RECAP PDF before asserting dollar amounts in a plan.
- **Meta v. Bright Data (Jan. 23, 2024):** Summary judgment for Bright Data on Meta’s contract theory for **logged-out public** scraping under then-current Facebook/Instagram terms (court found Meta failed to prove nonpublic/login-gated scraping). Not a universal ToS free pass.
- **X Corp. v. Bright Data (2024):** Alsup dismissed X’s complaint regarding scraping of publicly available posts / contract theories as pleaded (RECAP PDF excerpts). Later docket activity includes Bright Data antitrust counterclaims after X ToS changes (**separate order**). Fact-specific; X’s ToS have changed.

### C4. AI training fair use (2025–26)
- **ROSS (3d Cir. Sept. 29, 2026):** Affirmed that Westlaw headnotes are copyrightable and ROSS’s use to train a **non-generative, directly competitive** legal-research tool was **not** fair use. Explicitly ordinary copyright case; generative AI left open.
- **Bartz (June 23, 2025):** Purchased books scanned for LLM training analyzed favorably on fair use in that order; **pirated library retained as permanent general-purpose library not fair use**.
- **Kadrey (June 25, 2025):** Meta granted MSJ on fair use for Llama training on the record; court discussed market-dilution theory but found plaintiffs lacked proof; differs from Bartz on piracy framing.
- **Doe v. GitHub (Sept. 16, 2026):** §1202(b) output theory fails where model generates new works that never contained CMI; ordinary copyright remains.
- **NYT v. OpenAI/Microsoft:** Active; Aug. 6, 2026 order dismissed material-contribution contributory claims with prejudice after Cox; Sep. 4, 2026 cross-motions for summary judgment reported; **no fair-use merits ruling as of research date** (SECONDARY status blogs + docket PDF for Cox-related dismissal).

### C5. Console / emulator enforcement outcomes
- **Yuzu (PRIMARY judgment Mar. 6, 2024):** Consent to judgment; $2.4M; permanent injunction; judgment adopts Nintendo’s position that Yuzu is primarily designed to circumvent and play Switch games using unauthorized keys (RECAP Doc 11).
- **Ryujinx (SECONDARY Oct. 2024):** Lead developer reportedly contacted by Nintendo; GitHub org removed; **no public court opinion found** → UNKNOWN as legal holding; treat as enforcement risk signal.
- **Dolphin Steam (SECONDARY 2023):** Valve removed Dolphin from Steam after Nintendo dispute letter; Dolphin continued elsewhere. Not a litigated §1201 merits decision.
- **Nintendo DMCA Aug. 17, 2026 (PRIMARY):** Fork network takedown citing TPMs / prod.keys / Tropic Haze judgment.

### D2. SAS Institute (C-406/10)

### D3. DSM 2019/790 Arts. 3–4 (PRIMARY fetched)
- **Art. 3:** TDM exception for research organisations / cultural heritage institutions with lawful access for scientific research; copies may be retained securely for research/verification; **no rightholder opt-out** of Art. 3 itself (Art. 7 interaction beyond scope here).
- **Art. 4:** Broader TDM exception for lawfully accessible works for any purpose; **does not apply if rightholders expressly reserved** the use in an appropriate manner, **including machine-readable means** for content made publicly available online.

### D4. AI Act Art. 53 (PRIMARY fetched)
GPAI providers must, inter alia, **(c)** put in place a policy to comply with Union copyright law and **identify and comply with** reservations under DSM Art. 4(3) using state-of-the-art technologies; **(d)** publish a sufficiently detailed training-content summary. May rely on Art. 56 codes of practice until harmonised standards. Open-source GPAI exemption from some docs obligations does **not** apply to systemic-risk models.

### D5. GPAI Code of Practice (PARTIAL)
Official Commission hub confirms Copyright chapter exists (robots.txt / machine-readable opt-outs, lawful access, complaint channels in secondary PDFs). Treat Code as **compliance pathway under Art. 53(4)**, not itself copyright law.

### D6. Trade Secrets Directive Art. 3 (PRIMARY fetched)
Lawful acquisition includes observation, study, disassembly or testing of a product made available to the public or lawfully possessed **without a legally valid duty limiting acquisition**.

### D7. UK CDPA s.50B (PRIMARY fetched)
Lawful user may decompile low-level program to higher level if necessary to obtain information to create an independent interoperable program; info only for that objective; not if info readily available; not to create substantially similar expression. **Contract terms purporting to prohibit permitted acts are void (s.296A).**

## F. Contracts, robots, privacy

- **EULA anti-RE:** Enforceable under **Bowers** / **Davidson** in those circuits for assenting parties; UK/EU interoperability exceptions may void contrary terms (**CDPA s.50B(4)**; Software Directive Art. 8 style — confirm). Skill: if the relevant user or organization accepted anti-RE terms, flag YELLOW/RED even if copyright fair use would allow RE.
- **Browsewrap vs clickwrap:** Formation is fact-specific; X/Bright Data and Meta/Bright Data opinions discuss whether scraper was bound. Do not assume browsewrap binds.
- **robots.txt:** RFC 9309 — rules are **not** access authorization; still skill default to honor; stricter recipient rules require inspection.
- **GDPR / EDPB:** Opinion 28/2024 — legitimate interest needs three-part test; not automatic for AI training. Guidelines 03/2026 (July 2026) address web scraping for GenAI — **parse PDF before detailed claims**. Special-category data needs Art. 9 basis.
- **CCPA:** Personal information broadly defined; “sale”/“share” defined in §1798.140; publicly available exceptions exist but are narrow — scraping CA consumer data can trigger obligations. Prefer official leginfo/CPPA text.

## H. Persistence ladder (defensible routes + authority)

1. **Permissive alternative donor** — avoid copyleft/source-available conflict (license texts).
2. **Idea-only from public description** — Sega/Oracle/SAS support separating ideas/function from expression; still not a license to copy expression.
3. **License-compatible subset** — SPDX per-file.
4. **Documented public APIs / official SDKs** — contract + copyright clearer than RE.
